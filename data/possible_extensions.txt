Notes on reliability and limitations (short and practical):
- Government datasets (USGS, FEMA, NRCS, NOAA, NREL, EPA) are generally the most authoritative and traceable — use these for regulatory and design decisions whenever available.
- Community or private datasets (OSM, commercial geocoders, Maxar/Google imagery) are excellent for situational awareness and practical work but aren’t the legal record in most jurisdictions.
- Spatial resolution and update frequency matter: e.g., USGS 3DEP/LiDAR >> SRTM; FEMA FIRMs can be years out of date; SSURGO can be heterogeneous within a mapped unit.
- For engineering-level decisions (foundations, grading, floodproofing, contamination), these datasets are the right first step — but they do not replace site-specific field tests, borings, survey control, and local permitting checks.
- US-only vs global: soil/flood/seismic detailed tools that I use are primarily U.S.-focused (NRCS, FEMA, FEMA NFHL, USGS NSHM). Climate and geocoding services have good global coverage, but soil and regulatory flood data are best inside the U.S.
